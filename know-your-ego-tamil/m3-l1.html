    Recognising the Middle Ground: Healthy Ego

    Healthy Ego vs. Inflated Ego vs. Low Self-Worth

    📖 Detailed Explanation

    A healthy ego is quietly confident, not loud. It can say “I was wrong” without falling apart, and “I did well” without needing applause. It doesn't need to be the smartest person in the room, and it doesn't hide in the corner either. The sign of a healthy ego is that your self-worth stays steady whether you are praised or criticised — because it comes from within, not from other people's opinions.

    🌳 Metaphor

    A healthy ego is like a tree with deep roots. The branches (opinions, praise, criticism) may sway in the wind, but the trunk stays firmly in place.

    👤 Real-Life Example

    When Arjun's business proposal was turned down by investors, he felt disappointed for a day. Then he asked for specific feedback, changed the plan, and presented it again the next month — without losing his sense of self-worth.

    💡 Practical Tips
    • Ask yourself daily: “Did my mood today depend on what someone else thought of me?”
    • Celebrate wins quietly to yourself before telling others — this builds your own inner confidence.
    • When criticised, separate the useful message from the harsh tone of the person saying it, and let go of the tone.
    🧭 Coach's Guidance

    Practice the “24-hour rule”: after any big win or setback, wait 24 hours before making a major decision or announcement. This stops both over-confidence and crushed self-worth from controlling your choices.

    Live Worksheet

    Reflection Exercise

    Recall a recent piece of praise and a recent piece of criticism. Did either one change how you felt about your own worth? Why or why not?
