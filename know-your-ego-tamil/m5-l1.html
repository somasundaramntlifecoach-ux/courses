    Ego and Credit: The Silent Team Killer

    Ego at the Workplace

    📖 Detailed Explanation

    One of the most damaging forms of workplace ego is not wanting to share credit. A leader with an unmanaged ego takes ownership of team wins and distances themselves from team failures, openly or quietly. Over time, the team feels this deeply, even if it's never talked about openly — it destroys trust, motivation, and honest communication faster than almost any other workplace problem.

    🌳 Metaphor

    A leader who keeps all the credit is like a farmer who takes the whole harvest home but expects the workers to keep planting seeds for free next season — eventually, no one wants to plant anymore.

    👤 Real-Life Example

    A team lead, Deepak, presented a client-ready dashboard to senior management as “his” work, without mentioning that a junior analyst had built 80% of it. Within two months, that analyst quietly stopped sharing new ideas in meetings — the damage to motivation had already happened, long before anyone raised the issue.

    💡 Practical Tips
    • In every team update, name the specific people behind specific contributions.
    • When something goes wrong, use “we” language before pointing at any one person.
    • Once a week, say one sentence in public that gives credit to someone junior to you.
    🧭 Coach's Guidance

    Once a month, review your recent messages (emails, presentations, meeting notes) and count how often you used “I” versus “we” or a colleague's name when talking about successes. Try to steadily shift that balance toward shared credit.

    Live Worksheet

    Reflection Exercise

    Think about your last team success. Did you (or your manager) give credit to everyone involved? What is one thing you could do differently next time?
