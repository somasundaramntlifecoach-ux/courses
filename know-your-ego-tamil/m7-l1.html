    Common Ego Trigger Categories

    Recognizing Your Ego Triggers

    📖 Detailed Explanation

    Ego triggers usually fall into a few common types: being corrected in public, being compared to someone else, having your ability questioned, being interrupted, being left out of a decision, or getting advice you didn't ask for. Once you can name your own top three triggers, you gain the ability to see them coming — and a trigger you can see coming loses much of its power over you.

    🌳 Metaphor

    Knowing your ego triggers is like knowing which roads in your city flood every rainy season — you can't stop the rain, but you can plan a different route and avoid getting stuck.

    👤 Real-Life Example

    Karthik realised, after writing in a journal for two weeks, that his biggest ego trigger was being compared to his older brother. Once he named this pattern, he could notice the tightness in his jaw the instant a comparison started, and he chose not to react defensively at the next family gathering.

    💡 Practical Tips
    • Keep a simple trigger log for two weeks: what happened, how your body felt, how you reacted, and what you wish you had done instead.
    • Rank your top three triggers and prepare one calm response for each in advance.
    • Share your top trigger with someone you trust — saying it out loud reduces its power over you.
    🧭 Coach's Guidance

    Create a simple two-column “Trigger Map”: Column 1 lists your top triggers, Column 2 lists a planned response for each (“When X happens, I will do Y”). Keep this somewhere visible for the first month of practice.

    Live Worksheet

    Reflection Exercise

    List your top 3 personal ego triggers. Next to each, write one calm response you could use the next time it happens.
