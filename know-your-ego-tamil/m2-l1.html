    The Parent Ego State: Rules and Judgment

    The Three Ego States: Parent, Adult, Child

    📖 Detailed Explanation

    The Parent ego state is like a recording of attitudes and behaviours we picked up from our own parents and other authority figures. It has two forms: the Critical Parent, which judges, corrects, and lectures (“You should have known better”), and the Nurturing Parent, which supports and cares (“Let me help you with that”). Both are useful in the right moment. But a person stuck in Critical Parent mode becomes controlling and harsh in everyday life.

    🌳 Metaphor

    The Critical Parent is like a strict schoolteacher who never left your head. Decades after school ended, this “teacher” is still marking every action as right or wrong.

    👤 Real-Life Example

    Suresh grew up hearing his father say, “Real men don't cry.” Now, whenever his own son gets emotional, Suresh hears himself saying the exact same words — a Critical Parent recording playing automatically, even though he doesn't personally agree with it.

    💡 Practical Tips
    • Notice when you use words like “should,” “must,” or “always/never” — these are typical Critical Parent phrases.
    • Ask yourself: whose voice am I actually hearing when I judge myself or others harshly?
    • On purpose, switch to Nurturing Parent language. Instead of “You should have,” say “Next time, you could try.”
    🧭 Coach's Guidance

    Make a short list of phrases your parents or teachers often used. For each one, decide clearly: keep it, soften it, or drop it. This turns an automatic habit into something you choose on purpose.

    Live Worksheet

    Reflection Exercise

    Write down one phrase from your childhood that you still catch yourself repeating. Will you keep it, soften it, or drop it — and why?
