    Listening to Respond vs. Listening to Understand

    Ego and Communication Patterns

    📖 Detailed Explanation

    Ego-driven listening is really just waiting for your turn to speak — the brain is busy building a reply while the other person is still talking. Real listening means setting the ego aside for a moment, and staying curious about the other person's point instead of defending your own. This shift alone solves a surprising number of conflicts, because most arguments are really about feeling unheard, not about the actual topic.

    🌳 Metaphor

    Listening to respond is like two radio stations broadcasting on the same channel at the same time — both signals turn into noise, and neither message actually gets through.

    👤 Real-Life Example

    During a team disagreement, Sindhu noticed she was mentally preparing her reply while her colleague was still explaining his concern. She stopped, said, “Let me make sure I understand you fully before I respond,” and repeated his point back in her own words. He visibly relaxed — he simply wanted to be heard before any solution was offered.

    💡 Practical Tips
    • Before replying in a tense discussion, repeat the other person's point in your own words first.
    • Notice if you are forming your reply before the other person finishes speaking — that's a sign the ego has taken over your listening.
    • Ask one genuine follow-up question before sharing your own opinion.
    🧭 Coach's Guidance

    For one week, in every disagreement, follow this rule before sharing your view: repeat the other person's position back to them and ask, “Did I get that right?” Only continue once they confirm. This single habit trains ego-driven listening into real understanding.

    Live Worksheet

    Reflection Exercise

    Recall a recent conversation where you felt unheard, or where someone else might have felt unheard by you. What would 'listening to understand' have looked like there?
