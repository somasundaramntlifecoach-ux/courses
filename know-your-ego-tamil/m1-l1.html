    The Two Faces of Ego: Protector and Prisoner

    What Is Ego? Understanding the Concept

    📖 Detailed Explanation

    Ego is the part of the mind that answers the question “Who am I?” It holds our identity — our roles, our achievements, our beliefs, and how we see ourselves. In simple terms, ego is how strongly we protect our self-image. A healthy ego gives us confidence and a steady sense of self. An unhealthy ego becomes stubborn and defensive. It always needs praise from others to feel safe.

    🌳 Metaphor

    Think of the ego as a security guard at the door of your identity. A good guard checks people kindly and lets in what is useful. A guard who is too strict turns everyone away and fights with anyone who comes near — leaving you alone, even when there was no real danger.

    👤 Real-Life Example

    Ravi, a 42-year-old bank manager, was explaining a spreadsheet mistake to a junior colleague. The colleague politely said that a formula was actually correct. Ravi suddenly felt angry and insisted he was right, even after seeing the correct answer. That evening, thinking back on it, he realised he wasn't defending a fact. He was defending his image as “the manager who never makes mistakes.”

    💡 Practical Tips
    • Notice the body signals (heat in the face, tight chest, fast heartbeat) that come just before you react defensively. This is your ego's alarm bell.
    • Separate the fact being discussed from your self-worth. A mistake in a spreadsheet does not lower your value as a person.
    • Practice saying “You may be right, let me check” as your first response in disagreements.
    🧭 Coach's Guidance

    If you feel you must “win” every conversation, write down these moments in a small notebook for one week. Most people are surprised at how often the ego takes over normal conversations. Simply noticing this, without judging yourself, is the first and most powerful step to change.

    Live Worksheet

    Reflection Exercise

    Describe one recent moment when you felt the urge to defend yourself even though no real threat existed. What was really being protected — a fact, or your self-image?
